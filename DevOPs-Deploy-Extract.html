01

Policy compliance & rebate eligibility

From policy review to claim eligibility.

Built a connected Foundry workflow for validating payer policy compliance against pharmaceutical contracts. Contract entry and approvals, monthly document uploads, AI-assisted evaluation, and standardized claims data come together to determine rebate eligibility without relying on separate manual reviews.

FoundryReactOSDKAIP LogicAutomatePythonDocument Retrieval
Document intake: duplicate detection ↗Policy evaluation: document retrieval ↗
Read the case studyClose the case study+

THE PROBLEM

Each contract covers multiple drugs, and each payer has many plan policies that can change monthly. To validate submitted claims for rebates, the team had to identify the plans involved, collect their policy documents, and compare coverage against the contract terms. With hundreds of plans across payer, contract, and product combinations, reviewing everything manually was not feasible. Reviews focused on the highest-claim-volume plans, while other plans received rebates without the same level of validation.

FIG. 01 / END-TO-END FLOWSCHEMATIC · NOT CLIENT DATA
  1. 01Define

    Enter contract terms and contracted drugs through a custom React contract editor.

  2. 02Approve

    Manage In progress, Pending approval, Approved, Rejected, and Suggested states, with notifications and audit logs for changes.

  3. 03Collect

    Upload monthly payer policy PDFs and check for identical or similar documents against the registry and the current batch.

  4. 04Prepare

    Extract document text, create chunks and embeddings, and preserve source references for retrieval.

  5. 05Evaluate

    Use AIP Logic and Automate with retrieval tools to compare policy evidence against contract terms and determine eligibility.

  6. 06Connect

    Standardize claims from different sources and incorporate eligibility results into the claims data used by the team.

DECISION 01

Make contract changes accountable.

Built a React editor and a state-based approval process so contract terms are entered and reviewed in one place. Notifications keep the team informed, and audit logs retain the history of changes instead of leaving decisions in separate exchanges.

DECISION 02

Remove repeated document work before evaluation.

Monthly uploads can include documents already received. Added fingerprint-based duplicate detection so users do not need to check every file manually, then prepared policy content for targeted retrieval rather than passing complete PDFs into every evaluation.

DECISION 03

Carry the decision through to the claims.

Connected contract-driven policy evaluation to the downstream claims workflow. Standardized data from multiple sources and incorporated the eligibility results, so the output is usable in claim validation rather than ending as a separate document report.

WHAT THIS ENABLES

The team saved more than 400 hours in Q3 2026 through the combined workflow. Contract approvals, document intake, policy evaluation, and claims preparation now follow one connected process. This reduces dependence on manual reviews limited to top-volume plans. The time saving belongs to the full workflow, not to retrieval or duplicate detection individually.

03

Master data management

Less manual research. One stewardship workflow.

Built a Foundry workflow for MDM cases that need steward review. Connected Foundry-hosted language models to live web search and extraction tools from an external provider, automating the research needed to assess potential matches. Brought processing previously spread across individual notebooks into one shared workflow.

FoundryAIPWorkshopPython FunctionsOntology SDKWeb Search & Extraction
Read the case studyClose the case study+

THE PROBLEM

The existing MDM system automatically matched around 60% of incoming entities. The remaining 40% required steward attention. Upstream processing supplied potential target records, but stewards still had to search the web for each case to determine whether a candidate matched. Processing also ran in notebooks on individual users' systems, leaving research, preparation, and review fragmented. The work addressed both problems: automate web research and establish a shared processing workflow.

FIG. 03 / END-TO-END FLOWSCHEMATIC · NOT CLIENT DATA
  1. 01Route

    Take unresolved cases from the existing MDM process, alongside their potential target records.

  2. 02Prepare

    Run the previously notebook-based processing in a shared Foundry workflow.

  3. 03Research

    Let Foundry models call live web search and page-extraction tools through an external provider.

  4. 04Assess

    Use the retrieved information to assess candidates and suggest a match where supported.

  5. 05Review

    Bring the case, candidate comparison, saved research, and suggested result to stewards in Workshop.

DECISION 01

Automate the research, not just the result table.

Connected Foundry language models to live web search and content extraction as tools. This addresses the repeated task of opening websites and gathering information for each unresolved case, rather than only presenting candidates for stewards to research themselves.

DECISION 02

Bring individual notebooks into a shared process.

Consolidated the processing previously run on individual users' systems into a Foundry workflow. Case preparation, candidate information, research, and review now follow a common path instead of depending on separate notebook runs and manual handoffs.

DECISION 03

Give stewards evidence they can review.

Connected the research to the case and candidate comparison in Workshop. Stewards can inspect saved evidence and suggested matches while retaining responsibility for review. Candidate filtering keeps each comparison scoped to its case, and displaying a suggested result does not count as human approval.

WHAT THIS ENABLES

Automated the web-research step and established a shared processing and review workflow in Foundry. Stewards spend less effort gathering information case by case and can focus on evaluating evidence and resolving uncertain matches. The existing MDM auto-matching process remains the first step; this workflow supports the cases it cannot resolve.

04

Policy compliance / document retrieval

Find the evidence. Keep the context.

The document retrieval layer within the Policy Compliance workflow. Combines keyword and semantic search to supply relevant formulary and medical-policy evidence for evaluating contracted drugs across pharmacy and medical benefits, supporting the eligibility results used in claim validation.

AIP LogicPythonOntology SDKVector SearchFunction Registry
Full workflow: Policy Compliance ↗
Read the case studyClose the case study+

THE PROBLEM

Full PDFs contain large amounts of unrelated information, but retrieving too little can miss a short drug-table entry, a legend, or a continuation paragraph. The task was to change how evidence is retrieved without replacing the existing compliance rules.

FIG. 04 / END-TO-END FLOWSCHEMATIC · NOT CLIENT DATA
  1. 01Index

    Document chunks with embeddings and page metadata.

  2. 02Scope

    Build queries from contract products for one document.

  3. 03Retrieve

    Union keyword and semantic matches; deduplicate.

  4. 04Investigate

    Search again, expand a chunk, or read a complete page.

  5. 05Evaluate

    Existing benefit-specific rules, reports, and human overrides.

DECISION 01

Use literal search where literal words matter.

Short drug names and restriction codes can rank poorly in semantic search. Combined product and legend keyword searches with two semantic passes, then deduplicated chunks and restored document reading order.

DECISION 02

Give the evaluator a way to investigate.

Built four follow-up tools for lexical search, semantic search, neighboring chunks, and complete pages. Added tool-use instructions and source markers so incomplete evidence can be expanded rather than guessed.

DECISION 03

Missing evidence is not evidence of absence.

Added guidance against treating a failed search as proof of noncoverage. Preserved separate pharmacy and medical workflows, structured outputs, brand/molecule lookup, downstream reporting, and human overrides.

WHAT THIS ENABLES

Published five reusable Python functions and integrated retrieval into the Policy Compliance evaluation workflow. The evaluator can find product-specific evidence, expand incomplete context, and preserve page references while applying the existing contract rules and human override paths.

5 toolsinitial retrieval + targeted follow-up

Targeted evidence retrieval with document boundaries, page references, and human review preserved.

05

Policy compliance / document intake

A different filename. The same document.

The duplicate-detection widget supporting monthly document intake for Policy Compliance. Teams can upload payer policy files without manually checking whether each document already exists. Browser-side PDF and DOCX fingerprints distinguish identical files, re-exported content, and near-identical documents against the registry and current batch.

TypeScriptReactOSDKPDF.jsfflateSHA-256MinHash
Full workflow: Policy Compliance ↗
Read the case studyClose the case study+

THE PROBLEM

The same document can arrive with different metadata, a different filename, or small content changes. Exact file matching alone misses re-exported documents; comparing all original document text is expensive and requires retaining much more data.

FIG. 05 / END-TO-END FLOWSCHEMATIC · NOT CLIENT DATA
  1. 01Read

    Load PDF or DOCX bytes in the browser.

  2. 02Extract

    Extract text and normalize case and whitespace.

  3. 03Fingerprint

    File hash, text hash, and a 128-value MinHash signature.

  4. 04Compare

    Registry fingerprints via OSDK plus earlier batch files.

  5. 05Classify

    Identical file, same content, similar document, or new.

DECISION 01

Start with the cheapest reliable check.

Compare SHA-256 file hashes first, then normalized-text hashes. The second check catches documents that were re-saved with different bytes but unchanged extracted text.

DECISION 02

Keep a fingerprint, not the entire shingle set.

Represent five-word text sequences with a deterministic 128-value MinHash signature. Signature agreement estimates Jaccard similarity; an 85% threshold flags near-duplicates rather than claiming an exact content match.

DECISION 03

Check the current batch as well as the registry.

Use compact stored fingerprints retrieved through OSDK and add earlier files in the upload batch to the comparison set. Classification runs in the browser without a server-side classifier.

WHAT THIS ENABLES

The widget provides a clear reason for each classification and can compare new uploads without retrieving the original files again. Exact duplication and estimated similarity remain distinct, making the result more useful than a single unexplained duplicate flag.

3 checksfile · text · similarity

Three complementary checks catch duplication that filenames alone cannot identify.

02 / HOW I THINK

The interface is
only the surface.

What matters is whether the data is trustworthy, the logic is clear, and the next step makes sense to the person using it.

Bring me the problem ↗
01

Start with the decision.

Who needs to act? What do they need to know? A KPI alert, a record comparison, and a compliance report need different paths through the same platform.

02

Question the repeated work.

I look at where people spend time copying, checking, or waiting. Before automating a step, I ask whether it needs to exist at all. The aim is less work for the team, not another tool to manage.

03

Choose what fits, not what is newest.

I start with the simplest approach that solves the problem. Sometimes that is a clear rule; sometimes AI helps. I weigh the benefit against the effort to maintain it and keep people involved where their judgment matters.

04

Build the whole path.

Connect ingestion, ontology, application behavior, and testing. Improve the part that needs to change without breaking the workflows people depend on.
